# The 5 P's — positioning before content

Framework: Matt Gray. "Most founders don't have a content problem. They have a positioning
problem." The five pieces connect into one system: Positioning → Promise → POV → Pillars →
Platform. The outcome is a consistent system that builds trust, grows an audience and drives
revenue, and content stops being a daily obligation.

## Step 0 — collect facts before writing a word

The plan is only as good as the facts under it. Before drafting:

1. Grep the machine for what the brand already produces: skills, prompts, project folders,
   READMEs, posts, resumes. What they make every day is the positioning evidence.
2. For a person, read `.claude/skills/about-me/SKILL.md` and the confirmed-skills ledger
   first; never claim a skill, employer or result not in them.
3. List what is missing: founder or owner name, place, results that can be proven,
   channels and handles with current counts, price and paid products, who signs the content.
4. Ask once for the missing facts if the user is present. If not, write the plan with a
   **Gaps to confirm** section at the end and no proof points that depend on them.

## Mode A — diagnose an existing brand

Score each P as pass, weak or missing, with the evidence, then name the one P to fix
first. Order of blame when opportunities are not arriving: Positioning before Promise before
POV before Pillars before Platform. Posting more is never the first fix.

| P | The test | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Different enough to be memorable, relevant enough to matter, believable enough to trust | Uses the category's words ("best", "expert", "leading"); could be any competitor's bio |
| Promise | What happens because someone follows: specific outcome, time-bound, transformational | "Insights", "tips", "value"; no outcome a follower could check |
| POV | Lessons, beliefs, frameworks and non-obvious ideas earned through experience | Restates information anyone can search; no opinion a competitor would disagree with |
| Pillars | A few subjects to become known for, gone deep; pen or camera chosen, formats mastered | Seventeen topics; two channels started at once; format changes weekly |
| Platform | Post → awareness → authority → audience → owned asset | No owned list; the audience lives on one rented platform; nothing to buy at the end |

## Mode B — apply the framework

Produce one document with these sections, in this order, each short:

1. **Positioning**: one sentence naming the space to own. Then the three-row table
   (different, relevant, believable) with how this brand passes each, from evidence. Then
   "what to stop saying".
2. **Promise**: one sentence, marked *draft to confirm*. Show the three properties
   (specific outcome, time-bound, transformational) and state what it does not promise.
3. **Point of view**: three to five beliefs the brand already acts on, written in its voice.
   Each must be non-obvious in its market and earned through something it has done. These
   are the content IP.
4. **Pillars**: pick two. Pen (X, LinkedIn, newsletter, PDF) or camera (YouTube, Instagram,
   shorts), the format, the channel, the cadence, in a table. Name the second medium and
   the condition for starting it (a consistency streak, not a date). Name subjects not to
   chase.
5. **Platform**: the four-stage table, awareness → authority → audience → asset, with what
   each stage is for this brand and one measure per stage. State how the free thing funds
   the paid thing.
6. **What this changes on Monday**: five or six concrete actions, one call to action per
   post, four weekly numbers to track.
7. **Gaps to confirm**: every fact the plan needed and did not have.
8. One line attributing the framework to Matt Gray and stating the plan is a proposal.

## Rules

- No invented results, follower counts, founders, cities or prices. A gap is listed, not
  filled. A plan with an honest gaps section beats a plan with a made-up proof point.
- The promise stays inside what the product can deliver. Coverage, not selection; practice,
  not placement.
- Every POV point must trace to something the brand has actually done; if it cannot, cut it.
- Two pillars, not three. If the user wants a third, that is a second plan in ninety days.
- Save the plan as `<BRAND>_5Ps.md` in the brand's working folder, dated in the first line.
- Nothing is posted, published or changed on any channel by this skill. It writes the plan.
- For her own brand, run `/personal-voice` on the POV section before it is used anywhere.
